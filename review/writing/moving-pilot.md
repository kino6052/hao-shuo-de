# Pilot: a reading text for lesson 11 (Space 2 — Moving)

The first text taken through the six steps of [WRITING_PLAN.md](../../WRITING_PLAN.md) by hand (W13, W14), on 2026-10-15. Level: words and patterns taught up to lesson 11 (113 words). Status: **draft for the author's review**, version 5 (after the blind read, step 5b).

## The English (approved)

**A Friend from Far Away**

1. I got up early this morning.
2. Today my friend was coming to my home from her country.
3. Her country is very far away.
4. I went outside and waited by the road.
5. Lots of cars came and went.
6. Then a big bus stopped near my home.
7. My friend got off the bus.
8. "You've arrived!" I said.
9. "Yes, I've arrived. It was a long way," she said.
10. "Is your home far from here?" she asked.
11. "No, it's nearby. Let's walk," I said.
12. The bus started moving again, and we walked home.
13. We went in through the door and up the stairs.
14. She put her bag down and said, "I'm so happy to be here."

## Step 4. The checks

- **Dictionary words and hanzi**: all 25 sentences of the 21 lines pass `kb_check_form` (every syllable a dictionary word, the hanzi spell the words).
- **Words at lesson 11**: every word is taught by lesson 11; all 13 of lesson 11's new words are used (guó, kǒu, chē, lái, qù, dào, qǐ, zǒu, cóng, lù, wài, yuǎn, fùjìn).
- **Lesson 11 points**: every module of the lesson is practised (come-go, from, arrive, direction, move, far, road).
- **Parse at lesson 11**: every line parses with the patterns taught up to lesson 11, except the 5 lines with shuō: or wèn: (`speech-quote` has no lesson). Their quoted parts parse on their own.
- **The right reading**: at lesson 11 the first reading of every line without speech is the one the sketch means. With all 139 patterns, line 3 reads wrong first ("the home of [Anna will come from her country to me]", through `verb-de-person`, lesson 19), and so did the first draft of line 8 ("… zài wǒ-de jiā fùjìn bù dòng le": "a bus is at me"); at lesson 11, line 3 has only the right reading. **Parsing at the text's level is what removed the wrong first readings.** A few lines keep odd extra readings even at lesson 11 (line 19 can also be read "on top of us, went"), so the read-back still has to name the reading it means.
- **Speech lines, with `speech-quote` added at lesson 11**: all 5 parse. Three read right first; "Wǒ shuō: Nǐ dào le!" and "Tā shuō: Wǒ dào le." read first with le outside the quote ("[I say: you arrive] le"). The right reading is second. This is finding 6.

## What the pilot found

1. **Parse at the text's level, always.** The level filter alone put the right reading first in every line of this text. `kb_parse` today parses with all patterns and shows 3 readings; the writer needs the text's level and every reading.
2. **The structures list is mostly a by-product of the parse.** The patterns each line uses, the lesson of each, and the flags (no lesson, too late) came from the parse; only the "avoided" rows needed a second try. A tool can print W15's list.
3. **Speech has no lesson.** `speech-quote` (X shuō: …) is taught by no lesson, so dialogue fails at every level. The stories and the phrase book use it all the time.
4. **The lookup was the slowest part.** Searching the composites' English, then checking each form's words against the level, by hand. Tier 0 (the book's phrasings) answered 6 of the 20 concepts outright and shaped 3 of the rewordings, so it pays to search it first.
5. **A blind read-back needs a fresh reader, and it pays.** In one session Claude remembers the English, so its own gloss found nothing. The blind subagent (W18) found three lines to fix ("is from" or "is travelling from", an "up" with nowhere to go, "love at here"), confirmed the one weak idea (friend), and raised two things for other reviews (the bus composite, 他 for women). It costs one subagent per text.
6. **The quote can end too soon.** With `speech-quote` taught (simulated at lesson 11 and at lesson 6), every speech line parses, but the first reading often closes the quote early and hangs the rest outside it: "Tā shuō: Wǒ ài zài zhè-lǐ" → "[she says: I love] [is here]"; "Tā wèn: Nǐ chī fàn ma?" → "[she asks: you eat rice] ma". The right reading is there, but second. Proposed fix: the quote runs to the end of the sentence (a tighter rule, not a looser one, K22). A bare-verb answer after the colon ("Wǒ shuō: Chī.") does not parse; texts use a full sentence ("Wǒ shuō: Wǒ chī.").
7. **W9 and W11 can clash.** A needed idea with no word at the level (friend) can be described (W9) and still come back weaker, which W11 does not allow. Settled by W16.
8. **The read-back sees what the parser cannot.** All three fixed lines parsed, at the right reading. Their trouble was the sense a reader of Mandarin brings (cóng X lái as "is from X") and what the story had not set up (where "up" is). So the checks of step 4 do not replace step 5b.

## Notes for other reviews

- **The bus composite** (composite review): "hěn-duō-rén-de chē" reads first as "a car that belongs to many people". Another form may read better; to look at in the composite batch that has 公交车.
- **他 for women** (book conventions): the book writes 他 for every tā, so a story about Anna shows "he" in hanzi. The pinyin is fine. A question for the author: keep 他 everywhere, or write 她 when the text says the person is a woman.
