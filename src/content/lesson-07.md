```vocab
bù | not, no
huài | bad, negative, broken
duō | many, a lot, very
fùmǔ | parent, ancestor
yī | one, united
lìliàng | power, energy | | 力量
```

Not every adjective gets its own dedicated word. Hao-shuo-de builds "strong" out of two words you already know: <audio-example zh="有">yǒu</audio-example> ("to have," from Lesson 6) plus <audio-example zh="力量">lìliàng</audio-example> ("power, energy") — <audio-example zh="有力量">yǒu lìliàng</audio-example>, literally "to have power." Bound with `-de` the same way any description binds onto a noun, <audio-example zh="有力量的">yǒu-lìliàng-de</audio-example> modifies directly: <audio-example zh="有力量的男人">yǒu-lìliàng-de nánrén</audio-example> ("a strong man").

Adjectives can modify other adjectives or verbs by preceding them directly as adverbs (e.g., `hěn duō` for "very many").

> ### The Causative Rule
>
> To turn an adjective into a transitive action (such as transforming "good" into "to fix/improve" or "bad" into "to break"), you must use the standard Mandarin bǎ construction paired with biàn (to become/change):
>
> `Subject + bǎ + Object + biàn + Adjective`

```examples
Nǐ-de zuò-de hěn hǎo. | Your work is very good.
Shuǐ gěi wǒ lìliàng. | Water strengthens me / gives me energy.
Nǐ shì yǒu-lìliàng-de nánrén. | You're a strong man.
Zhīdào-de rén kàn xiě-de dōngxi. | The scholars read the document / look at the paper.
Xiǎo-de nǚrén méiyǒu hǎo-de tīng fùmǔ. | The girls misheard / didn't listen well to the parent.
Méiyǒu rén shì huài-de. | Nobody is bad.
Nánrén-de fùmǔ duō-de kàn xiě-de dōngxi. | Fathers use/read the book a lot.
```
